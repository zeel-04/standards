# Overlays

Modals and sheets both keep users in context without navigating to a new page. Choosing the wrong one creates friction — a modal that scrolls feels cramped; a sheet for a two-field form feels heavy. The decision rule is straightforward: fit determines format.

## Rules

1. Use a modal when the content fits on one desktop screen without scrolling.
2. Use a sheet when scrolling is required — longer forms, multi-step flows, or secondary editing panels.
3. Never nest a modal inside another modal; open a sheet instead.

## When to use each

| Pattern | Use when |
|---------|----------|
| Modal | Confirmation prompt, short form, single focused action that fits one screen |
| Sheet | Long or multi-section form, editing a record inline, content that requires scrolling |

## Do / Don't

**Do**
- Keep modal content short enough that no scroll is ever needed
- Use a sheet for secondary editing — e.g. opening record details alongside a list without losing your place
- Close a sheet by sliding it away; close a modal with a clear dismiss action or backdrop click

**Don't**
- Put long forms in a modal and let them scroll
- Open a new page for something that naturally belongs in an overlay
- Stack modals — if a triggered action needs more space, escalate to a sheet
